# Intensive Permanent-Bed Market Gardening

Small-scale commercial vegetable growing on limited land relies on **standardized permanent beds** so that tools, spacing, planning, and labor stay uniform across the whole plot.

- **Bed geometry.** A common standard is beds about **30 inches (75 cm) wide**, separated by pathways of roughly 18 inches (45 cm). Beds are given a fixed length (often 50–100 ft / 15–30 m) so seeding rates and expected yields can be planned per bed.
- **Permanent, no-till surface.** Permanent beds are **never walked on**, which preserves soil structure. Instead of plowing, the surface is loosened with a **broadfork** — deep tines that aerate without inverting the soil — and compost is applied on top.
- **High-density planting.** Tight in-row spacing plus succession sowing keep beds continuously productive and maximize yield per square meter.
- **Weed control without herbicide.** Deep compost or mulch, and **occultation** (covering a bed with an opaque tarp for several weeks), exhaust weed seeds before planting.

Because every bed shares the same dimensions, a grower can move transplant trays, irrigation lines, and row cover between beds interchangeably, and can express yields and labor as simple per-bed figures.
